## Impact Assessment

The proposed AI-Based Biomedical Waste Segregation Monitoring System successfully processed and maintained records for **78 biomedical waste detections** during the evaluation phase. The automated logging mechanism enabled centralized storage of detection details, eliminating the need for manual record maintenance.

Analysis of the detection logs revealed that **general waste constituted the highest proportion of detected waste items**, indicating its frequent occurrence within the monitored hospital environment. The generated **Waste Distribution and Bin Utilization Analysis** provided valuable insights into waste generation patterns and disposal practices, supporting the estimation of disposal requirements and optimizing waste handling strategies.

From the model performance perspective, **higher confidence scores were achieved for visually distinctive waste items**, demonstrating the effectiveness of the YOLO-based detection framework. In contrast, **cotton exhibited comparatively lower confidence levels** due to its irregular shape, texture similarity with surrounding materials, and relatively smaller object size, highlighting potential areas for future dataset enhancement and model refinement.

### Operational Impact

* Reduces dependence on continuous manual waste inspection and monitoring.
* Automatically maintains centralized records of biomedical waste detections.
* Enables visualization and analysis of waste generation trends.
* Supports estimation of disposal requirements and resource planning.
* Facilitates data-driven decision-making for efficient biomedical waste management within hospital environments.

Overall, the system demonstrates the potential of AI-assisted monitoring to improve the efficiency, traceability, and analytical capabilities of biomedical waste management practices in multi-speciality hospitals.
